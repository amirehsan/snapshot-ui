Add LineChart, with the `LineChartProps`, `LineChartLabels`, `LineSeries` and `LinePoint` types: one or two series (`series`, the first in ink and the second, for context, in slate), with `xLabels`, optional `xTicks`, `yTicks` gridlines with their labels, a `yDomain`, an optional area (`hasArea`) and a `height`, drawn on the server in SVG. Every label and value is your formatted string; `value` only places the point. The plot is one tab stop: Left and Right move a readout and a crosshair through the days with data, Home and End reach the ends, Page Up and Page Down move a week, Escape hides the readout, and a screen reader hears each day once the keys rest, as separate parts with no punctuation added between your words. Every chart has a toggle under it that swaps the plot for a table of the same strings, and with scripts off the table is shown under the plot; `labels` replaces its three strings, all three at once. Time follows the page's direction: in a right-to-left region the newest day is at the left and Left moves to newer days, from the nearest `dir="ltr"` or `dir="rtl"` attribute, which the drawing, the marks over it and the keys all read, so they agree even under `dir="auto"`. `null` points are gaps. `charts.css` draws it, and sets `--chart-direction` beside `--chart-flip`. React is the charts package's one required peer: it does not ask for `react-dom`, since no chart imports it.
