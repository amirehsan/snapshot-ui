## Header and body

The header holds a title and a note, ruled off from the body. The title is the card's heading. Long words break anywhere in a card; set `overflow-wrap: normal` on an element that must not break, such as a code sample.

## Actions in the header

Put a card's own actions in `CardActions` after the title and the note. Show at most two: for three or more, show the first and put the rest in a More menu, as an account's Details card shows Edit and keeps Merge and Remove in the menu. Beside the title they end its line, level with its first line however far the title wraps, and move down by the card's corner inset, so they keep their own shape and stand clear of the rounded corner, on the body's column edge: 7px in Rounded, nothing in Soft and Square. Where two buttons do not fit beside the title's 12rem they wrap among themselves at the end, and the note still follows the title. In a header narrower than 24rem nothing moves: the title and the note sit where they would without actions, and the actions take a row of their own after the note, at the start, so the order on screen is the order a screen reader reads. A label too long for the card wraps inside its button.

The header takes no width from its actions, so a card on a phone never scrolls the page sideways. A card that holds only a header and sits where it takes the width of its content, such as in a flex row, needs a width of its own.

## Nesting

A card inside another card, directly or in its body, takes a corner concentric with the outer card's, its radius less the outer card's edge and padding (7px in Rounded), and takes an outline. A card on a card has no tone step to set it apart. A control of your own in a card's corner, such as a button that ends its body, keeps its own shape and moves out of the corner instead: keep the card's `--holder-corner-inset` under it, so it stays on the column's edge, as the row under Header and body does with `margin-block-end: calc(var(--holder-corner-inset) - var(--holder-pad))`.

## Usage

Card is static. It renders on the server and ships no JavaScript, and so does CardActions: the buttons you put in it are yours. For a card that folds, use [Collapsible card](/docs/components/collapsible-card), whose `actions` prop draws the same way. Choose the element with `as` and the heading level with `level`, so the card fits the page's outline.

```tsx
import { Button, Card, CardActions, CardBody, CardHeader, CardNote, CardTitle } from '@snapshot-ui/react';

export function Notifications() {
  return (
    <Card as="section" aria-labelledby="notifications-title">
      <CardHeader>
        <CardTitle id="notifications-title" level={2}>
          Notifications
        </CardTitle>
        <CardNote>Where we reach you, and how often.</CardNote>
        <CardActions>
          <Button variant="secondary" aria-label="Edit notifications">
            Edit
          </Button>
        </CardActions>
      </CardHeader>
      <CardBody>...</CardBody>
    </Card>
  );
}
```

## Accessibility

A card is a div unless `as` says otherwise. A `section` named by `aria-labelledby` or `aria-label` is a region landmark; without a name it is not announced. An `article` is never a landmark.

The header is a div, not a header element. A header outside sectioning content is the page's banner, so two cards at page level would add two banners. The title renders the heading level you choose and looks the same at every level.

A card is not interactive. To make one lead somewhere, put a link in its title. Never nest buttons or links in a clickable card.

CardActions adds no role and comes after the title and the note, so a screen reader and the Tab key meet the actions after the words they act on. Name each button with what it acts on, "Edit details" rather than "Edit", since a page of cards holds many Edits. A button that opens a dialog gets focus back when the dialog closes.
