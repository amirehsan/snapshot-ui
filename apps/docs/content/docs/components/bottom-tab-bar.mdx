## Items and counts

A bottom tab bar links the pages an app opens most on a phone. It docks flush with the bottom edge on the float fill, with a hairline above and the safe area padded inside.

- Items: each `BottomTabBarItem` is an icon over a one-line label. The page shown is an ink pill.
- Counts: a small ink chip at the icon's corner, with its words in the name. The Transactions tab below reads "Transactions, 12 to review".

The examples on this page sit in a phone frame, so the bar docks at the frame's bottom edge, not the window's.

## The More button

An item without `href` is a button. Wrap it in a `SheetTrigger` with a [Sheet](/docs/components/sheet) of the other pages to open the sheet, and it reports whether the sheet is open. Or pass `onPress` for your own action. `isCurrent` on a button draws the pill but sets no `aria-current`, since a button is not the page shown and the sheet carries its own current link.

## Capacity

Use four short labels, or five at most, each under about 60px at 375px. Each label gets `(width - 8) / n - 8` pixels:

- Four tabs: 83.8px at 375px, 70px at 320px.
- Five tabs: 65px at 375px, 54px at 320px.

"Transactions" is 77.3px, so it fits four tabs from 350px and ellipsizes at 320px. On a phone, the frame around these examples is narrower than the screen, so it may ellipsize here but fit in your app. The label is one line, with an ellipsis as a last resort, and the accessible name always holds the whole label.

Label widths in Sofia Sans at 14px and weight 500: Transactions 77.3px, Documents 70.4, Accounts 57.5, Settings 50.9, Insights 48.4, Home 35.9, Goals 33.5 and More 30.6. So Home, Transactions, Goals and More fit at 375px.

## The safe area

The bar pads the safe area under and beside its items, through `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` and its left and right twins. On iOS these are 0 unless the page covers the whole screen. In Next.js:

```ts
// app/layout.tsx
import type { Viewport } from 'next';

export const viewport: Viewport = { viewportFit: 'cover' };
```

## Usage

CSS alone hides the bar from 1024px up, so it can stay in the page at every width.

- [App shell](/docs/components/app-shell): pass it as `bottomBar`. Below 1024px, the page keeps the bar's 64px and the safe area clear under its end.
- Routing: links navigate through React Aria's `RouterProvider`, which [Routing](/docs/routing) sets up once. `render` hands an item's anchor to your own link component.
- Item count: the bar warns once in the console with fewer than three items or more than five, and still draws them.

</Section>

## Accessibility

The bar is a `<nav>` named by its `aria-label`, holding plain links and buttons in tab order. It has no tab roles: these are pages, and "tab bar" is only its name in prose.

- The page shown carries `aria-current="page"`.
- Icons are hidden from assistive technology. A count's number and words are in the name.
- A button in a `SheetTrigger` reports whether its sheet is open.
- From 1024px up the bar is `display: none`, so it leaves the accessibility tree, and a rail's nav and the bar's never both appear.
