## Gauge, Meter or ProgressBar

A gauge is a measurement of the reader's state on a known range, which can go up or down: a credit score, a utilization, a budget spent, a savings goal saved. It is a [Meter](/docs/components/meter) bent round: Meter for a row, Gauge for a glance. Work the system is doing, such as an upload or an import, is a [ProgressBar](/docs/components/progress-bar), or a [Spinner](/docs/components/spinner) while its length is unknown. A screen reader announces a gauge as a meter, and an upload drawn as one would say the wrong thing.

A gauge is a ring, a full circle from the top, or a semicircle, an arc from the inline start over the top. It comes in three sizes: `sm` is 32px with its text beside it, `md` 96px and `lg` 160px with their text under them. The arc's length, its round ends included, is exactly the value's share of the range: nothing at 0, a dot under the stroke's width, half the track at 50% and the whole track at 100%.

## A score

A credit score runs from 300 to 850, so `minValue` and `maxValue` set the range, and 742 draws 80.4% of the arc, not 74.2%. `figure` is the numeral in the middle and `figureCaption` the word under it; neither has a default, since a percentage would be wrong on a score. Marks at 580, 670, 740 and 800 show the band edges, and their labels are read as the gauge's description.

Your app picks the tone. Fair and Poor are both `warning`: a low score needs a look, and it is not a failure, so it is never `error`. They are told apart by the word under the figure and by where the arc stops between the marks, never by color. A good score is ink, never green: the words say it is good.

The gauge draws no numbers at its ends. The example puts the range's ends, 300 and 850, under the semicircle's ends, and a legend of the bands under the scores, so a reader can place the arc without knowing the scale. Both are the app's, in its own words and digits.

## A share of a limit

A utilization runs from 0 to 100, the defaults. Marks at 10% and 30% show where a reader should stay under. `warning` says a state needs a look; above `maxValue` a neutral gauge draws warning by itself, its arc stops at the end, and `valueLabel` must say "over". `error` is only for a limit that stopped something, such as a declined payment.

## In a table row

A 32px ring fits a [DataTable](/docs/components/data-table) row in every density. The column header names the cell, so the gauge takes `aria-label` with the row's name, "Utilization, Everyday Visa ending 4821", and shows only its value text beside it. On a phone the ring moves to the item's meta line. A 32px gauge holds two or three marks; marks closer than the stroke's width overlap.

The marks are the same on every row, so give them an empty `label`: a mark with no words is drawn and not read, and a screen reader does not hear the thresholds again on each row. Say them once, in a note by the table, as the example does. A mark's gap is cut out of the arc, so the row's own fill shows through it, hovered or selected too.

## Usage

The gauge is static: a server component renders it with no client JavaScript, and its numbers are arithmetic on its props, the same on the server and in the browser. Pass every figure and word as a string; the library formats no numbers.

- `minValue` and `maxValue` set the range, 0 and 100 by default. A value outside it is drawn clamped.
- `valueLabel` says the state in words and is what a screen reader hears. At a warning or error tone, or over the limit, it says so.
- `figure` and `figureCaption` draw the numeral and its word at `md` and `lg`; there are none by default and none at `sm`. A figure never wraps or shrinks: keep it to 4 characters at `md` and 6 at `lg`, such as "100%" or "$12.3K". In a ring the caption sits in the circle where it narrows, so at `md` it is one line of about 60px, a word or two, and at `lg` two lines of about 104px; in a semicircle it is two lines under the chord. Past that it ends in an ellipsis, and the value text carries the words whole.
- `tone` is `neutral`, `warning` or `error`, your choice; there is no success tone.
- `marks` takes up to five; a sixth is not drawn. A mark with an empty `label` is drawn and not read.
- The arc moves over 200ms when the value changes, to and from 0 too, never on first appearance, and not at all under reduced motion. It follows the page's direction: on a right-to-left page a ring runs counter-clockwise and a semicircle fills from the right.
- Each mark stands in a gap cut out of the track and the arc, so whatever is behind the gauge shows there: a card, the page, a hovered or selected table row. It needs no surface color. `--gauge-size` on the gauge draws it at another size.

```tsx
import { Gauge } from '@snapshot-ui/react';

<Gauge
  label="Credit score"
  form="semicircle"
  size="lg"
  minValue={300}
  maxValue={850}
  value={742}
  valueLabel="742 of 850, Very good"
  figure="742"
  figureCaption="Very good"
  marks={[
    { value: 580, label: 'Fair from 580' },
    { value: 670, label: 'Good from 670' },
    { value: 740, label: 'Very good from 740' },
    { value: 800, label: 'Excellent from 800' },
  ]}
/>;
```

## Accessibility

The dial is a `role="meter"` from `minValue` to `maxValue`, named by the label, `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`.

- Its value is read as `valueLabel`, never as a number on its own. The visible value text, the figure and its caption are hidden, since the meter reads the same words, and the same said twice is noise.
- The marks' labels, in order of value, are read as its description.
- It takes no focus and has no keys.
- Chromium, Firefox and WebKit expose the dial to the platform's accessibility interface as a meter with its name, its value text and its description. What a screen reader then says for the role differs between readers and versions and is not yet verified here, so the value text carries the state in words.
