## Accordion, CollapsibleCard or Tabs

An accordion is a stack of rows. Each row is a title that shows or hides the section under it. Folding hides content, so use one only for what most readers can skip: answers to questions they may not have, or the sections of a long settings form that they visit one at a time. Content most readers need belongs on the page, open. GOV.UK's guidance is to test whether an accordion helps your readers before you add one.

<Preview name="accordion-basic" />

- When each section is a card with its own heading and actions, such as a Reset button, use a column of [CollapsibleCards](/docs/components/collapsible-card).
- When a reader chooses one view among several, such as a chart or a table of the same data, use [Tabs](/docs/components/tabs).

</Section>

<Section>

## One or several open

Several items may stay open at once by default, so a reader can compare two sections, and find-in-page or a field that fails never closes the section they were reading. With `allowsMultipleExpanded={false}`, opening an item folds the one that was open, and the two move together. Either way a reader may fold every item.

## Show all sections

`hasExpandAll` adds a "Show all sections" button above the items, at the end of the line. It opens every enabled item, then reads "Hide all sections" and folds them. A disabled item keeps the state it had. The button needs several items open at once, so with `allowsMultipleExpanded={false}` it is not drawn, and the accordion warns in development.

<Preview name="accordion-expand-all" />

The button finds the items among the accordion's own children, inside fragments and arrays too. An item wrapped in a component of your own still opens and folds, but the button leaves it out, and the accordion warns while `hasExpandAll` is on.

</Section>

<Section>

## Sections of a form

An accordion holds no value of its own. Fields in a folded section stay in the form and submit with it, since a hidden field still submits. A form's reset puts the fields back and leaves the sections as they are.

<Preview name="accordion-form" />

- A changed field in a folded section marks it with a clay dot after its title, as a folded [CollapsibleCard](/docs/components/collapsible-card) does. Pass `isChanged` to an item for a change no field reports.
- A field in a folded section that fails the browser's validation on submit opens its section at once, before the field takes focus. With one item open at a time, only the section of the first field that failed opens.
- With `validationBehavior="aria"` the browser fires no `invalid` event, so open the section yourself with `expandedKeys`.

</Section>

<Section>

## Nested accordions

An accordion inside an item works: each item follows its own accordion, and the arrow keys stay within the accordion they start in. Avoid it where you can, as GOV.UK and the Nielsen Norman Group advise. A heading inside the section usually serves the reader better than a second level of folding.

</Section>

<Section>

## Printing

Folded sections print open, with no chevrons and no Show all button, so a printed page of answers or settings has every section. In Chromium this is tested; in Safari and Firefox it is not yet verified, and if a browser will not print a folded section open, it prints the page as the screen shows it.

</Section>

## Usage

Give every `AccordionItem` an `id`, which `defaultExpandedKeys`, `expandedKeys` and `onExpandedChange` use to name it. An item is folded unless its id is in one of them. Titles are `h3` by default; pass `level` from 2 to 6 to fit the page's outline, and the look stays the same. On a Persian page, pass `labels` for Show all's words and `changedLabel` on each item.

```tsx
import { Accordion, AccordionItem } from '@snapshot-ui/react';

export function Settings() {
  return (
    <Accordion defaultExpandedKeys={['billing']} level={2}>
      <AccordionItem id="billing" title="Billing address">
        <p>12 Rose Lane, Bristol.</p>
      </AccordionItem>
      <AccordionItem id="notifications" title="Notifications" note="Email, push and digest settings">
        <p>A weekly digest.</p>
      </AccordionItem>
    </Accordion>
  );
}
```

An accordion is a client component that a server component can render; the server's HTML holds every title, the open sections and their state. If you import the stylesheets one by one from `dist/components`, import `card.css` with `accordion.css`: the folded chevron's turn, which follows the page's direction, is set there.

</Section>

## Accessibility

Each title is a heading that holds a button, so the sections appear in a screen reader's list of headings. The button reads the title, then expanded or collapsed, and names its section with `aria-controls`. The note sits under the heading, outside the button, so the button's name is the title alone. The chevron is decoration. Each section is a `group` named by its title, React Aria's default, rather than a `region`: a page of many questions would otherwise be a page of many landmarks.

<Keys rows={[['Tab Shift+Tab', 'Moves through Show all, each title, and the content of open sections, in page order.'], ['Enter Space', 'On a title, opens or folds its section. On Show all, opens or folds every section.'], ['Down Up', 'On a title, moves to the next or previous enabled title of the same accordion, with no wrap.'], ['Home End', 'On a title, moves to the first or last enabled title.']]} />

- When a section folds while focus is inside it, focus moves to its title. Show all keeps focus.
- Find-in-page searches a folded section in browsers that support `hidden="until-found"`, and opens it at once at a match. The server's HTML hides a folded section completely until the page hydrates.
- What a screen reader says for a folded, changed section, its heading level and its note is not yet verified on VoiceOver and NVDA.

</Section>
