## Bar, line or stat tile

Pick the chart by the question it answers.

- A bar chart compares amounts across categories or periods: where the month's money went, merchant by merchant.
- A [line chart](/docs/charts/line-chart) shows a change over continuous time: spending day by day.
- A [stat tile](/docs/components/stat-tile) says one figure, with a sparkline at most.

</Section>

<Section>

## A ranked list

Lay the bars down with `orientation="horizontal"` to rank categories, merchants or accounts. Each row has its name at the start, its bar from the zero line, and its amount at the end, in a column as wide as the widest amount, so the amounts align.

- Order: the chart draws the rows in your order. Sort them by amount, show the top eight to twelve, and sum the rest as "Other", last.
- Amounts: on by default for a horizontal chart. `valueLabels="none"` drops the column, and a list of row indexes shows only those rows' amounts.
- Gridlines: `valueTicks` draws a line at each value, with its label under the last row. The zero line is always drawn.

## Below zero

A row can net below zero, such as a category whose refunds outweigh its spending. Its bar runs from the zero line toward the start, and its amount stays in the end column, so a sorted list's negatives at the bottom still line up.

- Zero line: in the boundary color when the bars cross zero, so the reader finds it. With no negatives it is a quiet edge.
- Color: a negative bar is drawn in its series' color, ink here. Never red: error means a failure, and a refund is not one. The sign and the figure say which way it went.
- Domain: the value axis always includes zero, since a bar's length is its value. A `valueDomain` that leaves zero out is widened to include it, with a warning in development.

## Months

Bars stand up by default: one band per month, oldest at the start, each bar from the zero line. A bad month hangs below the line by itself, since the value axis always includes zero.

- Time bands: `bands="time"` labels the first, middle and last month, on one line, and Tab lands on the newest month first. `categoryTicks` names the months to label instead.
- Thickness: a bar takes 60% of its band at most and is never thicker than 24px, so twelve months in a phone's card are 12px each and thirty-one days about 5px, with nothing measured.
- Value labels: off by default, since the table, the readout and each bar's name carry every figure. `valueLabels` with band indexes labels the months the story is about, outside each bar's end: above a bar above zero, below one below it. Under 30rem `valueLabels="all"` keeps only the first and the last band's.
- Gridline labels: `valueTicks` labels each line at the start of the plot, in a column of its own as wide as the widest label, so no bar ever covers one.

## Two series

Two series sit side by side in each band, 2px apart, with a legend that names both. Without `color` the first is ink and the second slate, a story and its context. The month view pins its colors by meaning instead: Income is `color: 'gain'`, money that arrived, and Spending is `color: 'ink'`. Spending has no color of its own, red or otherwise, since nothing went wrong. Pinned colors also keep each series' color when your control shows one of them alone.

The types take two grouped series at most, so a third is a type error rather than a crowded band.

## Against the previous period

Give the previous period's series `look: 'hatched'` and its period's name as its `label`. Without a `color` it takes the first series' color, drawn as stripes inside a border of that color at full strength, and its amounts are slate: the same thing, last time, never a second category. Each bar's name says its period, so the stripes are never the only cue, and the keys step from this period's bar to the last one's in each band. Put the previous period second, so it stands after the current bar, or under it when the bars lie down.

Why stripes and not a lighter shade: a lighter fill must clear 3:1 against the card to be seen and stay apart from the solid bar beside it. Ink has a band that does (half strength measures 3.45 against the card and 5.17 against solid ink in light), but `gain` has none in either mode: at 50% it is 2.33 and 3.09 in light. The hatched bar's border is the color at full strength, 17.84 for ink and 7.20 for `gain` in light, and its stripes are a texture. In forced colors the previous period stays striped against the solid current bars. A bar under 4px across, such as a month of days compared in a phone's card, is its outline alone, since stripes that thin are noise; a comparison of more than about fifteen bands reads better lying down.

## Color

A series' color says what it is, by meaning first and then by identity. `color` takes `'ink'`, `'slate'`, `'gain'` or one of the [chart palette](/docs/theming#chart-palette)'s four, `'chart-1'` to `'chart-4'`, and pins the series to it.

- One series: ink.
- Two series side by side: ink and slate, a story and its context, unless you pin them.
- Money that arrived: `gain`, labelled so, beside an ink series and never beside a palette color, so a meaning and an identity never share a chart.
- Peers with no meaning of their own, such as a month's top categories: the palette's teal, violet, olive and magenta in that order, and an "Other" you sum and label in slate. Pin each series to its slot, so it keeps its color when a filter removes another.

No series takes error, success, warning, clay, signal orange or taupe; the type has no name for them. Amounts, labels and the legend's names stay ink and slate whatever a bar's color, and the legend's key beside each name carries the color.

</Section>

## Long names

A long name wraps in the label column, which takes the longest name's width up to 40% of the chart. Names are never cut short or rotated. Set `--bar-chart-label-size` on the chart to give the column another share.

When the chart is narrower than 24rem, each name moves to a line of its own above its bar, and the bar and its amount share the line below. The chart reads its own width, not the viewport's, so it behaves the same in a narrow card on a wide screen.

Standing up, a category label is as wide as its band and wraps to two lines, then ends with an ellipsis; under 30rem it keeps to one line, and under 24rem a chart of more than eight bands shows every third label. The whole name is always in the readout, the bar's name and the table. Names longer than about twelve characters, or more than eight categories, read better lying down.

The chart is a size container, so it takes its width from what holds it and none from its content: a block gives it the whole line, and in a flex row its item needs a basis, such as `flex: 1 1 20rem`, or a grid column a size. Given none, it is no wider than nothing.

</Section>

## States

`status` draws a state in the rows' place, at the height of the rows the chart expects: pass the categories you will show, or the chart keeps room for four.

- Loading: still bars in Skeleton's fill, with no legend, toggle or table. The chart says "Loading" once, politely.
- Empty: a sentence in slate over the zero line, or your `statusContent`, such as an EmptyState.
- Error: a sentence in the error color, which means a load failed, or your `statusContent` with a way to try again. The chart says it once.

## Usage

```bash
pnpm add @snapshot-ui/charts @snapshot-ui/core
```

It needs the same 0.x minor of `@snapshot-ui/core` (0.7 for charts 0.1.4), whose `styles.css` styles it. BarChart is a client part, and its server HTML is the final picture: every bar's place and size, the labels, the amounts, the legend and the table. The client adds the tab stop, the keys, the pointer and the readout. Nothing is measured, so the page does not move when it hydrates.

- Formatting: every figure is your string. `valueLabel` is what the reader sees, and `value` only places the bar. Currency, compact figures and Persian digits are yours, and the chart never sums, sorts or formats.
- `labels` and `statusLabels`: in another language, pass all six strings the chart writes itself and all three of its states, so no English is left on the page.

```tsx
import { BarChart } from '@snapshot-ui/charts';

<BarChart
  aria-label="هزینه بر اساس فروشگاه، مهر"
  orientation="horizontal"
  categories={['هایپراستار', 'اسنپ', 'سایر']}
  series={[
    {
      label: 'مهر',
      values: [
        { value: 412.3, valueLabel: '۴۱۲٫۳۰ دلار' },
        { value: 186.75, valueLabel: '۱۸۶٫۷۵ دلار' },
        { value: 64.1, valueLabel: '۶۴٫۱۰ دلار' },
      ],
    },
  ]}
  labels={{
    showTable: 'نمایش جدول',
    showChart: 'نمایش نمودار',
    noScript: 'اسکریپت‌ها خاموش‌اند، پس داده‌های نمودار در جدول زیر است.',
    roleDescription: 'نمودار',
    bar: 'میله',
    total: 'جمع',
  }}
  statusLabels={{ loading: 'در حال بارگذاری', empty: 'برای این دوره چیزی نیست', error: 'نمودار بارگذاری نشد' }}
/>;
```

## Accessibility

The chart is a `figure` named by `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`, which the types require. Its bars are a group named like the figure, with the role description "chart" (`labels.roleDescription`), and the table is the full alternative.

- One stop: the chart is a single tab stop, the active bar, so eleven rows are never eleven stops. It becomes focusable once scripts run, and Tab comes back to the bar last active: first the newest band for time bands, the first band otherwise.
- Names: each bar is an image named by its table cells, the band's category and its value, and its series when there are two, such as "Groceries September $598.30". A screen reader says it on every move, with no live region, and with your strings only.
- Keys: the arrows move to the bar drawn in their direction, through a pair bar by bar. Standing up, Left and Right move and follow the page's direction; lying down, Up and Down do. The other pair is left to the page.
- Readout: standing up it sits above the band's highest bar when it fits and below its lowest otherwise; lying down, above or below the active row. It never covers a bar of its band, and it repeats the bar's name, so it is hidden from assistive technology.
- Pointer: the whole band or row is the target, not only the bar, and the others step back to 40% while a pointer holds one. A pointer never moves focus.
- Table: the Show as table toggle under the chart swaps in a table of the same strings, for everyone who would rather read the numbers. With scripts off it is shown under the chart.
- Forced colors: the bars take the reader's text color and the active band Highlight, and a previous period stays striped against them; the zero line between the signs is the text color, and the gridlines GrayText.
